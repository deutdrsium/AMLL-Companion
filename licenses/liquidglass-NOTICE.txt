-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ideas taken from other implementations
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nothing below is copied verbatim. Each was read from the named project's source,
understood, and reimplemented against this library's own distance field, bevel
profile and uniforms. They are listed because the ideas are theirs and the
attribution is owed either way.

chrisbanes/haze — Apache License 2.0
  Copyright 2025 Christopher Banes and the Haze project contributors
  https://github.com/chrisbanes/haze

  The dome field for elongated shapes. Where a long capsule's refraction band
  reaches its own centre line, the distance-to-edge field collapses and its
  gradient loses direction. Haze blends the field into a smooth rectangular
  radius that is zero at the centre and one on every edge, with a closed-form
  gradient everywhere. See the domeWeight block in GlassShaderSource.kt.

Kyant0/AndroidLiquidGlass — Apache License 2.0
  Copyright 2025 Kyant
  https://github.com/Kyant0/AndroidLiquidGlass

  The closed-form gradient of a rounded rectangle, used here as a fast path in
  place of a central difference, generalised to this library's Ln corner; and the
  practice of evaluating that gradient against a corner radius wider than the
  outline's, so the normal rotates smoothly where the corner meets the flat run.
  See gradRoundRect in GlassShaderSource.kt.

QWEA0/Liquid-Glass-Android — MIT License
  Copyright (c) 2025 QWEA0
  https://github.com/QWEA0/Liquid-Glass-Android

  Two things. Rim softening: averaging the compressed rim image along the surface
  normal, so a hard boundary lands as a short gradient rather than as a single
  bright line that reads like a drawn stroke. And tint as an absorbing medium:
  multiplying by the tint rather than blending toward it, so the backdrop's own
  light and shade survive, with a small scattering term that keeps the hue
  readable over a dark backdrop. See uRimSoft and uTintAbsorb.

  Their per-angle measurements of the iOS 26 Control Center also corroborated this
  library's lighting model independently, and identified a direction-independent
  term in our edge line that should not have been there.

iyinchao/liquid-glass-studio - MIT License
  Copyright (c) 2024 Charles Yin
  https://github.com/iyinchao/liquid-glass-studio

  Compositing the rim highlight in a perceptual colour space, raising lightness and
  chroma rather than adding white. Adding white desaturates, so a lit rim over a
  saturated backdrop reads as a milky smear; raising lightness keeps the hue, and
  raising chroma alongside it makes the highlight more saturated than the ground
  beneath, which is what a specular on a tinted transparent medium does. Theirs is
  CIE LCH; ours is Oklab, for the cheaper transfer. See GlassOklabSource.kt.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Atlas stabilization and portable continuous lens (2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The continuous source map, calm response preset, scene host and portable web
adapter are authored by this project. The map is a polynomial identity-boundary
field, not Apple's private shader. Existing attributions above remain applicable.
Atlas's illustrative canvas artwork is generated by the sample; no external
astronomical image, live catalogue or SF Symbol asset is included.

The optional ports/skia adapter depends on CanvasKit (Google Skia), BSD-3-Clause.
CanvasKit is installed separately from npm; preserve its LICENSE and bundled
third-party notices when distributing its JavaScript/WASM runtime. The generated
SkSL bundle is this project's production source and retains the attributions above.

The optional ports/dotnet adapter depends on SkiaSharp (Microsoft/Xamarin), MIT.
The Windows Forms example also uses SkiaSharp Views and its OpenTK dependencies.
These dependencies are restored separately from NuGet. Preserve their LICENSE and
THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES files, including Google Skia's BSD license and native third-party
notices, when redistributing binaries. The adapter itself is Apache-2.0 and embeds
the same production SkSL source; no Apple runtime or assets are included.
